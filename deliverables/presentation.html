1. Motivation

H&M Group runs roughly 4,400 stores in 70-plus markets and a digital arm that competes head-on with Zalando, About You, Otto, Bonprix, and Breuninger across the DACH region. Personalisation is the default interface to the catalogue: home page, category landings, post-purchase upsell, and the weekly newsletter all live on top of a recommender that turns implicit signal (clicks, views, add-to-cart, eventually purchases) into a ranked list of items the customer is most likely to engage with next.

Three constraints define the architecture:
  • Sparse implicit feedback over a 105k-item catalogue and a 1.37M-customer base.
  • Latency budget under 50 ms per request at retrieval time.
  • Heavy cold-start on both sides: new fashion drops weekly, new customers daily.

The Kaggle competition H&M Personalized Fashion Recommendations (released by H&M Group, February 2022) packages exactly this problem. Predict the 12 articles each customer will purchase in the next seven days. Metric: per-customer Mean Average Precision at 12 (MAP@12) [Cremonesi 2010; Jarvelin & Kekalainen 2002]. The same architecture transfers without modification to Zalando and the rest of the DACH retail group because the constraints are the same.

2. Data

Three tabular files plus one image directory, roughly 30 GB once extracted. Phase 1 documents the dataset and does not download it. See data/README.md for the exact kaggle CLI command.

Sparsity and seasonality

Density of the customer-by-article interaction matrix: roughly 0.022%. Per-customer transaction count is heavily right-skewed (long tail of one-off shoppers, head of repeat customers with hundreds of purchases). Per-article count is similarly skewed and drives the popularity prior used in the two-tower logQ correction.

Fashion is brutally seasonal. The final week of the file (2020-09-16 to 2020-09-22) is reserved as the offline hold-out, mirroring the competition's public-test framing.

3. Methods - Hybrid Recommender

Two complementary models, blended at the top-100 retrieval stage with a fixed convex combination tuned on the last training week.

ModelFamilyInputsInference
model_baseline.py Implicit ALS [Hu, Koren, Volinsky 2008] customer x article confidence matrix, recency-decayed matrix lookup, top-12 via implicit's recommend
model_advanced.py Two-tower DSSM [Huang 2013; Yi 2019] user features + history, item metadata + optional image emb user-tower forward + Faiss HNSW top-k

Train / hold-out split

Mirrors the Kaggle metric. Train: transactions strictly before 2020-09-16. Hold-out: transactions in the final week (2020-09-16 to 2020-09-22). MAP@12, Recall@12, NDCG@12, coverage, and intra-list diversity are reported on the hold-out for both models.

4. Baseline - Implicit ALS

Why ALS is the right baseline: Hu, Koren, Volinsky's confidence-weighted ALS is the strongest single-model on transaction-only data. Rendle, Krichene, Zhang, Anderson [2020] showed a well-tuned MF matches or beats Neural CF on classical benchmarks. The Cython implicit library handles 1.37M x 105k in single-digit minutes on 16 cores.

Confidence weighting and recency

Per (customer, article) pair, the confidence is C_ui = 1 + alpha * w_ui, where w_ui is a recency-decayed sum of purchases with a 30-day half-life: w = sum 0.5 ^ (days_old / 30). alpha = 40, factors = 128, regularisation = 0.05, iterations = 20.

Output

Dense user-factor matrix U and item-factor matrix V; score for (u, i) is U_u . V_i. Top-12 per customer with already-purchased items filtered.

Cold articles get scored via a popularity baseline taken over the last 14 days of training, segmented by garment group, because pure ALS has no item-side features.

5. Advanced - Two-Tower Neural Recommender

Architecture

Score factorises as f_u(x_u) . f_i(x_i), with each tower projecting to a shared 64-dim space. Item embeddings are precomputed once over the catalogue and indexed in Faiss HNSW [Malkov & Yashunin 2018; Johnson 2019]; per-user retrieval is one user-tower forward plus one ANN query, well under the 50 ms latency budget.

TowerInputsStack
User last-20 purchase ids (mean-pooled), club_member_status, fashion_news_frequency, age (z-scored) concat -> MLP(256) -> ReLU -> MLP(256) -> ReLU -> Linear(64) -> L2-normalise
Item 11 discrete-id columns (product type, group, colour, section, garment group, ...) + optional 512-d image emb 11 embeddings concat (+ image) -> MLP(256) -> ReLU -> MLP(256) -> ReLU -> Linear(64) -> L2-normalise

Loss - sampled softmax with logQ correction

Per batch: 4096 in-batch positives, 1024 uniform global negatives [Yang 2020]. Each logit is corrected by subtracting log Q(item) (empirical popularity), per Yi et al. 2019, to remove popularity bias from the in-batch sampler. Cross-entropy against the single-class label that picks the positive logit. AdamW, lr 1e-3, weight decay 1e-6, 3 epochs.

Inference

Item embeddings precomputed once, indexed in twotower_faiss.index (HNSW M=32, efSearch=64). Per-customer top-12 in single-digit milliseconds on CPU.

Optional reranker (Phase 3): SASRec [Kang & McAuley 2018] or BERT4Rec [Sun 2019] reorders the top-100 candidates with the customer's recent session. Out of scope in Phase 1.

7. Discussion

Why two-tower factorisation

A monolithic deep recommender [He 2017; Cheng 2016] is unusable at retail scale because retrieval needs one forward per (user, item) pair. The two-tower factorisation gives up some interaction-level expressiveness in exchange for the ability to precompute item embeddings once and run an ANN query at inference. This is the architecture that ships in production at Google for YouTube [Covington 2016], at Pinterest for PinSage [Ying 2018], and at Airbnb for search ranking [Grbovic 2018].

Negative sampling

Pure in-batch negatives are free but biased toward popular items. Pure global uniform negatives are unbiased but information-poor. The Yang 2020 mix - in-batch plus uniform global - is both informative and approximately calibrated when paired with the Yi 2019 logQ correction. The Phase 1 design uses exactly this mix.

Cold-start

Cold customers go to a popularity baseline (no signal exists). Cold articles go to content-only retrieval through the item tower (the right move because the item tower can score articles it has never seen in transactions, given metadata) [Schein 2002].

Fairness, calibration, diversity

Calibrated recommendations [Steck 2018] and aggregate diversity [Adomavicius & Kwon 2012] are reported alongside accuracy to confirm the system is not collapsing into a narrow popular subset of the catalogue.

What is out of scope in Phase 1

  • Sequential reranker (SASRec / BERT4Rec) - Phase 3.
  • Postcode-to-region embeddings - additional preprocessing required.
  • Online bandit on the blend weight, daily incremental retrain, sharded ANN service.
  • Multi-task heads (MMoE) [Ma 2018], graph CF (LightGCN, NGCF) [Wang 2019; He 2020].

8. Conclusion

The Phase 1 deliverable specifies and scaffolds a hybrid recommender for the H&M Kaggle competition that combines an implicit-feedback ALS baseline with a two-tower neural retrieval model trained with sampled-softmax-with-logQ over in-batch and mixed negatives, indexed with Faiss HNSW for sub-50 ms inference. Code is runnable end-to-end, references are CrossRef-verified, the manuscript is in manuscripts/manuscript.md. Phase 2 runs the full pipeline on the Kaggle dataset and reports the headline MAP@12 figures.

The architecture transfers without modification to Zalando, About You, Otto, Bonprix, and Breuninger because the constraints (sparse implicit feedback, large catalogue, tight latency, recency-driven content) are the same across the DACH retail group.
